# Audit

## What this bundle is

- **Question:** How can a Live Linux USB be booted on a 2013–2014 Intel MacBook to run
  open-source data-recovery tools, and which tools can perform an HFS+ deep scan (find
  deleted files that are no longer listed in a directory)?
- **Method:** Manual evidence collection with the built-in `websearch`/`webfetch` tools,
  plus two provider-backed Tavily searches. The bundle was written manually rather than
  generated by the generic `just research` pipeline, because the pipeline’s default
  subquestions are broad and this question turns on source-level HFS+ behaviour, specific
  Mac firmware, and one tool that is not in the roundup.
- **Date:** 2026-11-06.
- **Bundle:** `research/2026-11-06-macbook-hfs-deleted-recovery/`.

## Commands and tools actually used

- Built-in `websearch` for the query set in `queries.jsonl` (43 entries).
- Built-in `webfetch` for the LinuxLinks page, the Plop hfsprescue manual, the TSK HFS
  wiki, the Gentoo MacBook pages, the rEFInd installation page, the SystemRescue package
  list, the Kaisen tools page, and the Debian `blkls`/`fsstat` man pages.
- `curl` to fetch TSK source files (`hfs.cpp`, `hfs_dent.cpp`, `hfs_journal.cpp`) from

  `ScanBuffer()` and the scan loop.
- `just secrets-check` confirmed `TAVILY_API_KEY` is configured.
- `just search "HFS+ deleted file recovery hfsprescue Linux open source deep scan"` —
  Tavily, 1 credit; results were generic (ddrescue, Super User, forums).
- `just search "boot Linux live USB MacBookPro11,1 2013 EFI rEFInd"` — Tavily, 1 credit;
  results were generic rEFInd/Ubuntu guides, consistent with the primary sources.
- `jq` to validate `sources.jsonl`, `queries.jsonl` and `plan.v1.json`.

No worker or long-lived process was started. No application code outside `research/` was
edited.

## What the sources establish

1. **HFS+ deletion removes the catalog record and frees blocks; it does not leave a
   deleted directory entry.** The data survives until overwritten, and stale catalog
   records can survive in unused B-tree nodes. [s18][s19][s20]
2. **`hfsprescue` is the only turnkey open-source tool found that scans for deleted HFS+
   catalog records.** Its source reads every block and searches for catalog key/data
   patterns, then reconstructs names, parent IDs, CNIDs, sizes and extents. It supports
   HFS+ compression and runs on Linux/macOS/FreeBSD. [s06][s07][s09]
3. **TestDisk cannot undelete HFS+.** Its undelete list is FAT/exFAT/NTFS/ext2; HFS+
   support is partition/boot-sector repair. [s02][s03]
4. **The Sleuth Kit cannot enumerate deleted HFS+ names.** It walks the live catalog, marks
   every reached entry allocated, and its HFS journal functions are stubs. `icat` works
   with a known CNID; `blkls` extracts unallocated space. [s11][s12][s13][s16][s17]
5. **PhotoRec/Foremost/Scalpel are carvers.** They recover content by signature, not
   names, and lose fragmented/resource-fork/compressed data. [s04][s05][s10]
6. **Most of the roundup is not an HFS+ scanner.** ddrescue/safecopy/OpenSuperClone/
   DDRescue-GUI are imaging; Mondo/ReaR are backup/DR; Rescuezilla/Redo/Mariposa/
   SystemRescue are live distributions; RevLix is a GUI wrapper. [s01][s37][s39][s40][s41][s42][s46][s47][s48][s52]
7. **A 2013–2014 MacBook with an internal SSD that ran High Sierra or later is probably
   APFS, not HFS+.** Apple’s conversion is automatic and cannot be opted out. [s31][s32]
8. **TRIM on Apple internal SSDs makes deleted-file recovery a gamble.** The controller
   may return zeros; external USB drives are more likely to retain data. [s35][s36]
9. **The Mac boot path is UEFI-only for Linux.** Option at chime, “EFI Boot”, GPT/FAT32
   ESP, firmware password blocks it; rEFInd is the fallback; Ventoy 1.1.14+ has a Mac
   regression. [s22][s24][s25][s26]
10. **Removing the Apple PCIe SSD is the cleanest physical path.** OWC lists the relevant
    2013–2014 models as compatible. [s30]

## What remains uncertain

- **Actual filesystem of the user’s Mac.** The report branches, but only the machine’s
  `diskutil`/`fsstat` output can decide. This is the single biggest unknown.
- **Whether TRIM has already destroyed the deleted blocks.** The mechanism is established;
  the timing on a specific 2013–2014 Apple SSD is not. No model-specific measurement was
  found.
- **`hfsprescue` accuracy.** The author says it restores deleted files; the source shows
  the mechanism; but no independent benchmark of name/path/content recovery rates was
  found. False positives and partial files are expected.
- **HFS+ journal recovery.** The DFRWS method is established, but no maintained open-source
  implementation exists; TSK’s code is stubs. The report does not recommend a tool that
  does not exist.
- **APFS deleted-file recovery.** TSK’s APFS support is limited and encryption support is
  incomplete; snapshots are the reliable path, but not every Mac has them. Carving is the
  fallback.
- **Ventoy/Mac behaviour.** The regression is version- and model-specific; other
  combinations may work.
- **2013–2014 SSD interface details.** The PCIe AHCI description is community-supported;
  aftermarket drives/firmware can differ.
- **`hfsprescue` packaging.** It is in Kaisen and the AUR, not in Debian/Ubuntu main; users
  may need to compile it or use Kaisen.
- **The exact HFS+ compression methods.** TSK supports zlib and some LZVN; `apfs-fuse`
  supports ZLib and LZVN; LZFSE may not be covered everywhere. A compressed deleted file
  may be recovered but unreadable.

## Conflicts found and how they were resolved

1. **“TSK recovers deleted files” vs HFS+ source code.** TSK’s general description says
   deleted and hidden content is shown, and its NTFS/FAT/ext support is real. For HFS+,
   the source marks every reached catalog entry allocated and the journal is stubbed. The
   report follows the source and the 2010 OSDFCon deck, not the general marketing claim.
   [s11][s12][s13][s14][s34]
2. **“TestDisk supports HFS+” vs “TestDisk undeletes HFS+”.** TestDisk supports HFS+ for
   partition/boot-sector repair, not file undelete. The report separates the two. [s02][s03]
3. **“PhotoRec supports HFS+” vs “PhotoRec recovers HFS+ files with names”.** PhotoRec
   supports HFS+ as a block source, not as a metadata source. The report calls it a carver.
   [s04]
4. **“Deep scan” in Rescuezilla vs the word’s commercial meaning.** Rescuezilla’s “deep
   scan” menu item is PhotoRec, a carver; TestDisk is the “file recovery” item. The report
   uses “metadata scan” vs “data carving” to avoid the ambiguity. [s39]
5. **“APFS is HFS+ compatible” vs “the volume is HFS+”.** APFS can read/write HFS+ volumes,
   and HFS+ can read/write APFS on High Sierra, but that does not make an APFS volume
   HFS+. The report treats them as distinct on-disk formats. [s31][s32]
6. **“TRIM makes recovery impossible” vs “TRIM does not always zero immediately”.** The
   mechanism is real; the timing is controller-dependent. The report says the practical
   window is very small, not that recovery is metaphysically impossible. [s36]
7. **rEFInd vs Ventoy vs raw dd.** All three can work; the report recommends raw dd to a
   GPT/FAT32 USB first, rEFInd as the fallback, and Ventoy only with Secure Boot support
   disabled or version 1.1.12. [s24][s25][s26]

## Confidence

- **High:** the HFS+ deletion mechanics; TestDisk’s lack of HFS+ undelete; TSK’s lack of
  HFS+ deleted enumeration; `hfsprescue`’s scan mechanism; the Mac startup keys and
  firmware-password constraint; APFS conversion on SSD.
- **Medium:** `hfsprescue`’s real-world recovery rate; TRIM timing on these specific SSDs;
  the 15-inch GPU workaround; Ventoy version behaviour; the OWC enclosure compatibility
  for every SSD variant.
- **Low:** any claim that a specific tool will recover a specific deleted file on a
  specific Mac. The report deliberately avoids such a promise.

## Reproducibility

- Re-run the searches in `queries.jsonl`; the URLs in `sources.jsonl` are the exact pages
  used.
- The two indispensable primary sources are Apple TN1150 [s18] and the hfsprescue manual
  [s06][s07]; the decisive source-level findings are in `hfs_dent.cpp` [s12],
  `hfs_journal.cpp` [s13] and `hfsprescue.cpp` [s09].
- For the Mac boot path, Apple 102603 [s22] and the rEFInd installation page [s24] are the
  core; Ventoy issues #3733/#3774 [s25][s26] document the regression.
- The TRIM and APFS caveats should be re-checked against the specific machine; they are
  the parts most likely to change the plan.

## What would improve the answer

- A controlled HFS+ recovery benchmark (known files, known deletions, `hfsprescue` vs TSK
  vs PhotoRec vs commercial tools).
- A model-specific TRIM test on a 2013–2014 Apple SSD.
- A maintained open-source HFS+ journal parser.
- A 2026 re-test of Ventoy/rEFInd on MacBookPro11,1 and 11,3 with current ISOs.
- A report from someone who actually ran `hfsprescue` on a Mac-created HFS+ volume with
  compression and resource forks.
