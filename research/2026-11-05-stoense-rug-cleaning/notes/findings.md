# Findings summary

1. **The label is not contradictory.** “Do not wash” means no machine washing/full immersion;
   IKEA’s own FAQ says “Do not machine wash.” “Do not dry clean” is separate. “Professional
   carpet cleaning” is the water-based carpet-cleaning trade, not a dry cleaner and not a
   rug-washing plant.
2. **The face fibre and the backing have different limits.** Polypropylene tolerates water and
   resists most stains, but it is oleophilic (oil-loving), heat-sensitive (~150 °C softening),
   and prone to crushing. The synthetic rubber backing is the reason for “do not wash”: wet
   latex/SBR-type backings lose strength and can delaminate, especially with heat, folding, or
   slow drying.
3. **“Professional” is a process.** IICRC S100 / CRI 204: pre-inspection, pre-vacuum, spot
   treatment, pre-spray, dwell, agitation, extraction, rinse, controlled drying, post-
   inspection. Hot-water extraction is not true steam; low-moisture extraction is the relevant
   method for this rug.
4. **A private person can do most of it.** Tier 1: spot clean with foam and a damp cloth.
   Tier 2: whole-rug clean with a wet/dry shop vac + sprayer + soft brush + fan. Tier 3: rent
   an extractor (Silvan/Bauhaus/XL Byg, ~219–275 DKK for 4 h/day). Tier 4: hire a pro.
5. **For this 1.33 m² rug, compare costs.** Pro cleaning is ~250–725 DKK; rental is
   ~316–415 DKK plus time. DIY is much cheaper only if you already own a wet/dry vac. Hiring
   may be the rational choice for a single small rug.
6. **The main risks are over-wetting, heat, oil-based solvents, detergent residue, and slow
   drying.** Do not machine wash, soak, steam-mop, dry-clean, tumble dry, iron, bleach, use a
   rotating brush, or use oil-based solvents. Dry flat with airflow on both sides; target
   6–12 hours and never put it back damp.
7. **Uncertainty:** IKEA does not name the backing polymer or define “professional”. The
   advice is conservative and class-level, not a test of this specimen.
