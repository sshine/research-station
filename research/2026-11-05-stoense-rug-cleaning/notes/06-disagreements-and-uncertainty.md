# Disagreements, conflicts, and uncertainty

## 1. “PP can be hosed down / fully saturated” vs IKEA “Do not wash”

- Generic polypropylene guides often say the fibre is fine with full saturation:
  - Rug Branch: “Polypropylene handles getting fully saturated without any fibre damage” and
    gives a hose-down deep-clean method.
    https://www.rugbranch.com/blogs/rugs/how-to-clean-polypropylene-rug
  - SoCal Rug Masters: “most polypropylene rugs can be washed: take the rug outside, hose it
    down…” https://www.socalrugmasters.com/how-to-clean-a-polypropylene-rug/
- IKEA says “Kan ikke vaskes” / “Do not wash”, and the product has a **synthetic rubber
  backing**.
- **Resolution:** both can be true. The face fibre tolerates water; the backed construction
  does not necessarily tolerate full immersion. The manufacturer’s construction-specific
  instruction outranks generic fibre advice. For this rug, do not hose it down or soak it.

## 2. “Steam clean” vs “never steam”

- Some sources say polypropylene can be steam cleaned:
  - Ochoco: “Generally, yes, if it’s done correctly… many professional rug cleaners routinely
    use low-moisture hot water extraction on polypropylene rugs.”
  - Zerorez: “Yes, professional steam cleaning olefin carpet can be done, but it is important
    to let the pros… handle that job.”
- Others say never steam:
  - Rug Branch: “Never steam clean: High-heat steam can permanently melt or distort
    polypropylene pile fibres.”
  - Sutherlandshire: “high-temperature steam from a steam mop or carpet steamer can partially
    melt and distort polypropylene pile.”
- **Resolution:** the word “steam” is used loosely. **True steam** (dry vapour, high
  temperature) is risky. **Hot-water extraction** (heated water injected and immediately
  extracted) at moderate temperature is the professional method. Do not use a steam mop,
  wallpaper steamer, or kettle steam. If a cleaner says “steam”, ask for the water
  temperature and whether it is HWE or vapour.

## 3. “Dish soap is fine” vs “residue causes resoiling”

- Many DIY guides recommend a teaspoon of dish soap in water.
- Trade sources warn that any detergent residue left in the pile attracts dirt:
  - Milliken: “Over application of detergent can lead to a detergent residue being left on
    the fibres, which will attract rapid resoiling.”
  - HydraMaster: residue can return to its concentrated alkaline pH as water evaporates; an
    acid/neutralising rinse helps.
- **Resolution:** dish soap is acceptable for a spot clean **if** it is clear, mild, used
  sparingly, and rinsed thoroughly. For a whole-rug clean, use a carpet-specific low-residue
  cleaner at the minimum dose, and do a plain-water rinse pass. “Less is more” is the safe
  rule.

## 4. “Professional rug cleaning” vs “professional carpet cleaning”

- Professional rug-washing plants often recommend **full immersion** for area rugs. Example:
  Brio Carpet Care: “In-plant full immersion is the only recommended cleaning process for
  rugs.” https://www.briocarpetcare.co.uk/blog/how-to-clean-a-rug
- That is appropriate for many wool/Oriental rugs, but it conflicts with IKEA’s “do not wash”
  for a rubber-backed synthetic rug.
- **Resolution:** IKEA’s phrase is “professional **carpet** cleaning”, not “rug washing”.
  For this rug, prefer an in-home low-moisture extraction service or a synthetic-rug
  specialist who can protect the backing. If a provider only offers full immersion, ask how
  they handle rubber-backed rugs; if the answer is vague, go elsewhere.

## 5. The specific backing polymer is undisclosed

- IKEA says only “100 % Syntetisk gummi”. It does not say SBR, natural rubber, TPR, or a
  blend.
- The delamination/water-sensitivity evidence is mostly about **latex/SBR-backed carpet**.
  The exact sensitivity of this rug’s backing is therefore an inference, not a measured fact.
- **Practical consequence:** be conservative. The manufacturer already says “do not wash”;
  the safe assumption is that prolonged moisture and heat are bad. If the backing looks
  intact and the rug is spot-cleaned and dried quickly, the risk is low. If the backing is
  damaged or the rug stays damp, the risk is high.

## 7. “Professional” is not a regulated term for this rug

- IICRC S100 is a industry standard, not a law. A “professional carpet cleaner” may or may not
  follow it.
- IKEA does not certify cleaners or define what “professional” means. The user has to ask
  about method, chemistry, temperature, extraction, and drying.
- **Confidence:** high on the interpretation of the care label; high on the process steps;
  medium on the exact backing sensitivity; medium on prices (snapshot, location-dependent).
