# Private-person protocol: professional process with household/rented equipment

This is a synthesis. The individual steps are supported by the manufacturer instructions,
the CRI/IICRC process, Kärcher’s extraction guide, and practical DIY guides. Where a step is
an inference, it is marked **[inference]**.

## Decision tree

- **One or two spots, no overall dinginess** → spot clean (Tier 1). Do not wet the whole rug.
- **Overall grey/dull, oily traffic area, or faint odour** → deep clean (Tier 2 or 3).
- **Backing cracked, peeling, sticky, or rug wrinkled** → do not wet-clean; spot clean only,
  or replace. **[inference from backing-delamination sources]**
- **Pet urine/odour that has reached the backing, or large/old stains** → professional
  assessment; a surface clean may not reach the source.
- **You want a guarantee or cannot risk the rug** → hire a professional (Tier 4).

## Tier 1 — Spot cleaning (no special equipment)

Tools: white paper towels or clean white cloths, a spray bottle, a soft brush or sponge,
lukewarm water, a mild clear detergent or a carpet spotter, a fan.

1. **Remove dry matter first.** Scrape dried residue gently toward the centre with a plastic
   card or the back of a spoon; vacuum. IKEA: “Tørre pletter fjernes straks ved at skrabe
   forsigtigt mod midten af pletten.”
2. **Blot, do not rub.** Absorb as much liquid as possible with paper towels, working from
   the outside in. IKEA: “Gnid ikke på våde pletter. Sug væsken op med køkkenrulle…”
3. **Test.** Apply the chosen solution to a hidden edge or the back; wait 5 minutes; blot with
   a white cloth. Check for colour transfer, backing softening, or pile change.
4. **Apply foam, not a flood.** Mix a small amount of mild detergent with lukewarm water and
   whip it to foam, or use a carpet spotter per label. Apply the foam to a cloth and dab it
   onto the spot. Bolius recommends thick foam because it lifts soil and prevents it from
   sinking into the backing:
   > “En tyk skum opsamler nemlig smudsen, og forhindrer i højere grad, at det synker ned i
   > tæppet.”
   URL: https://www.bolius.dk/kan-du-rense-dine-loese-taepper-i-sne-9943
5. **Dwell 1–5 minutes.** Do not let the solution dry on the rug.
6. **Agitate gently.** Use a soft brush or sponge in the pile direction; do not scrub hard.
7. **Rinse.** Blot with a clean cloth dampened with plain lukewarm water. Repeat until no
   soap residue remains. Residue attracts new dirt. Milliken:
   > “Over application of detergent can lead to a detergent residue being left on the fibres,
   > which will attract rapid resoiling.”
   URL: https://contenthub.milliken.com/api/public/content/4894c10e53d74f12ab2cdf1a6e2dbca1?v=ba662377
8. **Dry.** Blot with dry white towels; weight them for 10 minutes; air-dry with a fan. Do not
   walk on the spot until dry.
9. **For grease/oil:** use a drop of clear dish soap directly on the spot, work gently, blot,
   rinse. PP is oleophilic; avoid oil-based solvents (WD-40, lighter fluid, eucalyptus oil).
   Source: Sutherlandshire.
10. **For pet urine:** blot, then an enzymatic cleaner per label; do not saturate; rinse and
    dry. If the odour returns, the backing/pad may be contaminated; consider a pro.

## Tier 2 — Whole-rug deep clean with a wet/dry shop vacuum

This is the “no rental” version. It is suitable for a 1.33 m² rug if you already own a
wet/dry vacuum. If you do not own one, a rented extractor (Tier 3) is usually better than
buying a shop vac for one rug.

Tools: wet/dry shop vacuum with a wide nozzle or upholstery nozzle, clean garden sprayer or
spray bottle, soft brush, white towels, shower squeegee, fan(s), dehumidifier (optional),
pH-neutral/low-foam carpet cleaner or a small amount of clear dish soap.

1. **Prepare the room.** Remove furniture and the anti-slip underlay. Vacuum the floor.
   Work in a dry, well-ventilated room; avoid humid days.
2. **Pre-vacuum both sides.** Use the standard floorhead, never the rotating brush (IKEA).
   Slow overlapping passes; vacuum the back too, because grit collects there.
3. **Pre-treat spots** using Tier 1.
4. **Pre-spray lightly.** Fill a clean sprayer with lukewarm water and carpet cleaner at the
   label’s minimum dose, or a few drops of clear dish soap per litre. Mist a 1 m² section from
   20–30 cm away; the pile should be evenly damp, not saturated. **[inference: keep moisture
   low because of the backing]**
5. **Dwell 5–10 minutes.** Do not let it dry. Milliken recommends about ten minutes for a
   pre-spray; more than that and it begins to dry out.
6. **Agitate.** Work a soft brush through the pile in overlapping strokes. Do not use a stiff
   deck brush or a rotary machine.
7. **Extract.** With the wet/dry vac, use slow overlapping passes; then 2–3 dry-only passes.
   A shower squeegee can help push water toward the nozzle. SoCal Steam:
   > “Wet/dry vac: press firmly and move slowly; repeat until minimal moisture is recovered.”
   > “1 wet pass followed by 2–3 dry-only passes.”
   URL: https://www.socalsteamclean.com/carpet-cleaning/diy-deep-carpet-cleaning-solution/
8. **Rinse.** Mist plain lukewarm water lightly over the section; extract again. Repeat until
   the recovered water is clear and no suds appear.
9. **Dry flat, elevated, with airflow on both sides.** Blot with towels first. Lay the rug on
   a clean tarp or two sawhorses/drying racks; run a fan across it and, if available, a
   dehumidifier. Flip after 4–6 hours. Target **6–12 hours**, and do not put it back until the
   backing is dry. **[inference: 6–12 h target from DIY/trade guidance; the exact time depends
   on room humidity]**
10. **Finish.** When bone dry, vacuum both sides and brush the pile in one direction.

## Tier 3 — Whole-rug deep clean with a rented extractor

This is the closest DIY equivalent to professional HWE.

- Danish rental: Silvan “Tæpperenser, inkl. mundstykke”, 10 L clean / 9 L dirty tank,
  239.95 DKK for 4 hours or 274.95 DKK for a day; cleaning fluid sold separately (75.95 DKK
  for 0.5 L, 139.95 DKK for 1 L). Bauhaus/XL Byg around 219 DKK for 4 hours; deposit often
  520 DKK (refundable). Sources: Silvan
  https://www.silvan.dk/produkt/taepperenser-inkl-mundstykke-4-timer-8820-9851367 and Rentay
  https://www.rentay.dk/p/taepperenser-688111e88a5d76c239c56198.
- Use the **hand/upholstery tool**, not a rotating floor brush. Keep the solution flow low.
- Fill with **lukewarm** water and carpet cleaner at the minimum dose. Do not use the hot/
  steam setting if the machine has one. Ochoco:
  > “ask them to use a low-moisture hot water extraction process rather than excessive heat.”
- Pre-vacuum, pre-spray, dwell, agitate, extract, rinse, dry as in Tier 2.
- Make 1 wet pass and 2–3 dry passes per section; overlap; do not go over the same spot
  repeatedly “until it looks clean”. Over-wetting is the main failure mode.
- Empty and rinse the recovery tank; clean the nozzle; dry the machine before returning it.

## Tier 4 — Hire a professional

For this rug, this is often the rational choice because the rug is small (1.33 m²). Ask for
**in-home low-moisture extraction** or a **synthetic-rug specialist** with controlled drying.
Avoid a full-immersion rug wash unless the provider can explain how it protects the rubber
backing. See `03-professional-methods.md` for the questions to ask.

## Chemistry rules of thumb

- **Mild, clear, low-residue.** A pH-neutral carpet cleaner is safest. If using dish soap, use
  a clear, non-moisturising, non-bleach type and only a few drops; rinse thoroughly.
- **No chlorine bleach.** IKEA says “Tåler ikke klorblegning.” Even though PP is relatively
  bleach-resistant at the fibre level, the backing and any dyes are not a test surface.
- **No dry-cleaning solvent.** IKEA says “Tåler ikke kemisk rens.”
- **No oil-based solvent.** PP is oleophilic; solvents can leave an oil residue.
- **No fabric softener, no wax, no silicone polish.** These leave films that attract dirt.
- **Do not mix cleaners.** In particular, never mix bleach and ammonia.
- **Less is more.** Over-application is the most common cause of resoiling, stiff pile, and
  wicking. HydraMaster:
  > “some of the prespray or extraction detergent you use to clean the carpet is going to be
  > left behind in the carpet … use an acid rinse or neutralizing rinse solution, to make sure
  > … you neutralized that residue.”
  URL: https://hydramaster.com/the-7-most-common-misconceptions-about-carpet-cleaning-chemistry-part-4-of-7

## Drying rules

- **Dry both the pile and the backing.** The face can feel dry while the backing is damp.
- **Never dry flat on a non-breathable floor.** Raise the rug or use a tarp/racks; air must
  reach the underside. RugPadUSA notes rubber-backed rugs trap moisture against the floor.
- **Do not hang a wet rug by one edge.** The weight can stretch or delaminate the backing.
  Spincycle warns against wringing/twisting for the same reason.
- **Target 6–12 hours; investigate if >24 hours.** If the rug stays damp, increase airflow
  and reduce humidity; do not put it back.
- **Do not use a tumble dryer, iron, hot hairdryer, or steam mop.** IKEA explicitly forbids
  tumble drying and ironing; heat can deform PP and damage the backing.

## What not to do (summary)

| Don’t | Why |
|---|---|
| Machine wash / soak / full immersion | IKEA: “Kan ikke vaskes”; rubber backing weakens when wet |
| Dry clean / solvent spot-clean | IKEA: “Tåler ikke kemisk rens”; solvent can attack backing/adhesive |
| Steam mop / high-heat steam | Can melt/distort PP and stress the backing |
| Tumble dry / iron / hot hairdryer | Heat damage to PP and rubber |
| Chlorine bleach | IKEA: “Tåler ikke klorblegning” |
| Rotating brush vacuum | IKEA: use the standard floorhead; brush can fuzz/distort pile |
| Hard scrubbing / stiff brush | Distorts pile, drives soil deeper |
| Oil-based solvent (WD-40, citrus, eucalyptus) | PP is oleophilic; leaves oil residue |
| Over-wetting / leaving wet | Delamination, mould, odour, floor damage, wicking |
| Putting back before dry | Trapped moisture under rubber backing |
