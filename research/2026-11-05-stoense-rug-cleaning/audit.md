# Audit

## What this bundle is

- **Question:** How can a private person apply “professional” cleaning to an IKEA STOENSE
  130 cm low-pile polypropylene rug with a synthetic rubber backing, without highly
  specialised equipment?

  built-in web search/fetch tools. The bundle was written manually rather than generated by

  this question turns on one specific manufacturer instruction and one specific construction.

## What the sources establish

1. IKEA’s Danish and English care text is consistent: 100 % polypropylene face, 100 %
   synthetic rubber backing; do not wash, bleach, tumble dry, iron, or dry clean; vacuum and
   turn; spot-clean with a damp cloth and mild detergent; professional carpet cleaning when
   needed; use the standard floorhead, never the rotating brush.
2. IKEA’s own care FAQ uses the phrase **“Do not machine wash”**, which disambiguates “do not
   wash” as a prohibition on machine washing/full immersion, not on damp spot cleaning.
3. Polypropylene is hydrophobic, oleophilic, solution-dyed, and heat-sensitive (melting
   point ~160–165 °C; softening ~150 °C). It has poor crush recovery.
4. Latex/SBR-type carpet backings lose strength when wet; excessive water, heat, and
   aggressive extraction can cause delamination. The exact backing polymer of STOENSE is not
   disclosed.
5. Professional carpet cleaning is a process standard (IICRC S100 / CRI 204): pre-inspection,
   pre-vacuum, spot treatment, pre-spray, dwell, agitation, extraction, rinse, drying, post-
   inspection. HWE is not true steam; low-moisture extraction is the relevant method for a
   rubber-backed PP rug.
6. A private person can reproduce the process with a wet/dry shop vac + sprayer + soft brush +
   fan, or more closely with a rented extractor. The critical disciplines are minimal
   moisture, low heat, low-residue chemistry, thorough extraction, and fast drying.
7. For a 1.33 m² rug in Denmark, professional cleaning is roughly 250–725 DKK; a rental is
   roughly 316–415 DKK plus time. DIY is much cheaper only if a wet/dry vac is already owned.

## What remains uncertain

- **Backing polymer:** IKEA says only “100 % Syntetisk gummi”. The water/heat sensitivity
  evidence is class-level (latex/SBR-backed carpet), not a test of this rug.
- **Safe moisture/temperature limit:** IKEA does not publish one. “Lukewarm” and
  “low-moisture” are inferences from the material class and trade guidance, not a
  manufacturer specification.
- **“Professional” definition:** IKEA does not define it. The interpretation as water-based
  carpet cleaning (not dry cleaning) is supported by the separate “do not dry clean”
  instruction and by the professional standards, but it is an interpretation.
- **IKEA care PDF:** not parsed. The product page and FAQ are consistent, so this is a small
  gap.
- **Prices:** snapshot 2026-11-05; vary by store, distance, minimum charge, and pickup/
  delivery inclusion.

## Conflicts found and how they were resolved

1. **“PP can be hosed down” vs IKEA “do not wash”.** Resolved by distinguishing face fibre
   from backed construction. Generic fibre advice does not govern a rubber-backed rug.
2. **“Steam cleaning is fine” vs “never steam”.** Resolved by distinguishing true steam
   (high-temperature vapour) from hot-water extraction (heated water, immediate recovery).
3. **“Dish soap is fine” vs “residue causes resoiling”.** Resolved by limiting dish soap to
   spot cleaning, using it sparingly, and rinsing thoroughly; whole-rug cleaning should use a
   low-residue carpet cleaner.
4. **“Professional rug cleaning = full immersion” vs IKEA “do not wash”.** Resolved by
   preferring in-home low-moisture carpet extraction or a synthetic-rug specialist over a
   rug-washing plant. IKEA’s phrase is “professional carpet cleaning”, not “rug washing”.

## Confidence

- **High:** interpretation of the care label; the professional process sequence; the list of
  things not to do; the existence of a DIY path.
- **Medium:** the exact backing sensitivity; the precise safe temperature/moisture; the cost
  comparison (snapshot and location-dependent).
- **Low:** any claim that a specific product or machine is “IKEA-approved”; IKEA does not
  approve one.

## Reproducibility

- Re-run the searches in `queries.jsonl`; the source URLs in `sources.jsonl` are the exact
  pages used.
- The IKEA DK product page and IKEA care FAQ are the two indispensable primary sources.
- The IICRC S100 page and CRI 204 PDF define the professional process; the Ochoco and Rug
  Branch pages illustrate the PP-specific debate.
- The Danish prices should be re-checked before purchase; they are a snapshot.

## What would improve the answer

- A statement from IKEA naming the backing polymer and giving moisture/heat limits.
- A laboratory test of a STOENSE backing sample after wet cleaning and drying.
- A documented method statement from a Danish carpet cleaner experienced with rubber-backed
  PP rugs.
- A controlled comparison of a wet/dry vac vs a rented extractor on this exact rug.
