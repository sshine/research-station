# Source note — BBC News, "US and Israel were behind Stuxnet claims researcher" (2011)

- **Title:** US and Israel were behind Stuxnet claims researcher
- **Publisher:** BBC News
- **Published:** 4 March 2011
- **URL:** https://www.bbc.com/news/technology-12633240
- **Source type:** news report on a conference claim
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-07

## Why it matters

An early (2011) public attribution claim, before the 2012 NYT/WaPo reporting. It shows both the claim and the state of evidence at the time: Ralph Langner said the US was the "leading source" and Mossad was involved, but the BBC noted there was "no hard evidence."

## Exact quotes

> Israel and the United States created the Stuxnet worm to sabotage Iran's nuclear programme, a leading security expert has claimed.

> Speaking at the TED conference in Long Beach, California, Mr. Langner said: "My opinion is that Mossad [Israel's intelligence agency] is involved."

> However he speculated that Israel was not the main driver behind the creation of Stuxnet. "There is only one leading source, and that is the United States," said Mr. Langner.

> The United States and Israel have led an international campaign to halt Iran's nuclear programme, however there is no hard evidence to link either country to the creation of Stuxnet.

> Iran's Bushehr reactor is believed to have been on of the intended targets for Stuxnet. [Image caption]

> Earlier in the week Iran's Interior Ministry denied that Stuxnet had been responsible for a shutdown at the country's Bushehr nuclear reactor.

## Notes and caveats

- This is a claim by a researcher, not proof. The BBC explicitly says there was no hard evidence at the time.
- The Bushehr image caption is speculative early coverage. Later detailed reporting centres on Natanz. The report should mention Bushehr only to explain the "power plant" confusion, and should note Iran denied Stuxnet caused the Bushehr shutdown.
- "on of" is a typo in the BBC caption; quote as published.
