- **Before Snoooze became a travel sleep brand, it began with one very familiar problem: trying to sleep on a bad hotel pillow. Snoooze was founded by Danish designer Ann Sjøgreen Sanger, who spent years travelling between Fashion Weeks. Wherever she went, the same problem followed her: lumpy hotel pillows and restless nights. Ann knew she slept best on her own pillow. But carrying a full-size pillow was impractical, while the available travel alternatives didn’t feel like a real pillow. So, in 2019, she created Snoooze: a proper pillow that could pack down, travel easily, and bring a little of home into hotel rooms, planes and trains. That original idea still sits at the heart of Snoooze today: Good sleep away from home should still feel like real sleep. Since February 2023, Snoooze has been owned and run by Yev Lukash. In our next post, we’ll share why Ann’s idea became deeply personal to Yev and why a pillow can sometimes mean much more than comfort. Thank you, Ann Sjøgreen Sanger , for seeing an everyday problem and believing it deserved a better answer. #Snoooze #FounderStory #TravelSleep #SmallBusiness**

- **Last week I had the opportunity to attend the Technology Entrepreneurship Bootcamp 2026, organised by University of Exeter Engineering and led by Gigi Wang . I am proud to say my team won. Five days, a team of strangers on Monday, and by Friday we had built a venture, pitched it to industry judges, and come out on top. Our venture was Corner Coach, an all-in-one platform for martial arts gyms: a web dashboard for coaches to run members, payments, scheduling and compliance, and a mobile app for members to track their training. A few moments stuck with me. Sebastian Wood closed the week with a phrase I have taken to heart: always say yes. Arshan Iqbal , a fellow data scientist, showed how fast you can build a startup but how you cannot rush validation. David Sanger was honest about the discipline of knowing when to cut your losses. And a full-circle moment, learning from Raphael Dennett , who first taught me entrepreneurship as an undergrad here at Exeter. My own biggest takeaway: a strong idea counts for little without execution under pressure. Over five days the difference between teams came down to clarity, making the value obvious in two minutes, and the trust to let your teammates make your idea better.
