# Smart plugs compared – which ones actually help you save energy

Every smart plug can switch a lamp. Far fewer measure what the appliance uses, and fewer still let an energy manager read that measurement and switch the plug when your solar panels are producing or electricity is cheap. We compared nine smart plugs sold in Europe on exactly that.

## What a smart plug is good for in an energy system

A plug is the cheapest piece of hardware that can both measure and control a single appliance. In the Zerofy app it does three jobs.

### Find out what things cost

Most households are wrong about where their electricity goes. An hour with a measuring plug on the freezer, the aquarium or the old second fridge usually settles the argument – and standby loads that run 8,760 hours a year add up faster than people expect.

### Run appliances on solar surplus

When your panels produce more than the house needs, Zerofy switches the plug on instead of exporting the surplus for a few cents. A dehumidifier, a pool pump, a towel rail or a second water heater are ideal – they do not care when they run, only that they run.

### Follow a dynamic tariff

On a dynamic tariff the price changes every hour. Zerofy knows tomorrow's prices and can move the same flexible loads into the cheapest window, without you watching a price curve.

## Six things to check before you buy

The differences that matter are not the ones on the packaging. These are the ones that decide whether a plug is still useful in a year.

### Does it actually measure?

A plug that only switches tells you nothing. Look for power in watts and a cumulative energy total in kWh – the Philips Hue and Amazon plugs, for example, switch but do not measure, which makes them useless for this job.

**Tip:** Check the spec sheet for "power metering" or "Energiemessung", not just "smart".

### Enough amps for the load

Ratings here run from 10 A (2300 W) to 16 A (3680 W). A fan heater, a kettle or a 2 kW immersion heater will sit close to the limit, and a plug run permanently at its maximum gets warm.

**Tip:** For heaters and water heaters pick 13 A or 16 A. For a fridge, a pump or a TV, 10 A is plenty.

### An open interface

This is what separates a plug you can automate from one you can only tap. A documented local HTTP or MQTT API, or a cloud API your energy manager supports, means something other than the manufacturer's app can read and switch it.

**Tip:** Shelly and myStrom both document their local APIs. Zerofy connects through the manufacturer cloud in both cases.

### Does it need a hub?

Wi-Fi plugs work on their own. Zigbee needs a Zigbee hub, DECT-ULE needs a FRITZ!Box, and Thread needs a Matter controller that is also a border router. None of that is a problem if the hub is already in your hallway – it is an extra purchase if it is not.

**Tip:** Count the hub in the price if you do not already own one.

### Matter is not the whole story

Matter makes pairing easy and keeps a plug working if the manufacturer's cloud is switched off. But the Matter electrical-measurement cluster is not yet widely implemented, so in most controllers you get on/off and sometimes live power – not the energy history.

**Tip:** Treat Matter as insurance against vendor lock-in, not as a replacement for a proper API.

### Indoors or outdoors

Almost every plug on this page is indoor-only, rated 0 °C to 40 °C and explicitly not for damp rooms. A pond pump, garden lights or anything on a balcony needs an IP44 model built for it.

**Tip:** An indoor plug in a weatherproof box is not the same thing – the plug itself has to be rated.

## Questions we get asked

### Can a smart plug make my fridge or my heating smarter?

Not really, and in the case of a fridge you should not try. Cutting power to a compressor on a schedule shortens its life and risks your food. Smart plugs belong on loads that genuinely do not mind being interrupted – dehumidifiers, pumps, towel rails, water heaters, chargers, lights. For a heat pump, use its own interface or an SG Ready contact instead.

### How accurate are the measurements?

Good plugs state ±1 % over most of their range; Shelly and myStrom both do. Accuracy falls off at very low loads, so a 2 W standby reading is a rough indication rather than a number to build a bill on. None of these plugs is a calibrated meter and none may be used for billing a third party.

### Do I need a smart plug if I already have a smart meter reader?

They answer different questions. A P1 dongle or optical reader tells you what the whole house is doing, in real time, which is what solar surplus control needs. A plug tells you what one appliance is doing and can switch it. Most people who care about this end up with one meter reader and two or three plugs.

### Why does Zerofy only support Shelly and myStrom?

Because both publish a documented API and are happy for third parties to use it. Where a manufacturer keeps control inside its own app, an independent energy manager cannot switch the plug at the moment it matters. If that changes for another brand, we will add it.

### Can I use a smart plug to measure my balcony solar system?

Yes, and it is a common setup. A metering plug between the microinverter and the socket gives Zerofy the production figure for a plug-in solar system that has no monitoring of its own. Check your inverter's output stays within the plug's rating.

### What about Tasmota, ESPHome and Home Assistant?

If you flash your own firmware you already have a local API and can do most of this yourself. Shelly plugs are the easiest route because they ship with a local API and you do not have to open anything. Nothing on this page needs flashing.
