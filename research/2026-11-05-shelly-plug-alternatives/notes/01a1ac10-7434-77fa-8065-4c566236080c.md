# Wi-Fi rescue plug

Leave a prepared Shelly plug with the customer. When the router is replaced, it brings every Shelly back online so you can move them to the new network.

Updated: October 2026

## What you need

* **A mains-powered Shelly Gen2 or Gen3 plug**, for example a Shelly Plug M Gen3.
* **The customer’s Wi-Fi name and password**, exactly as they are.
* **Devices with Shelly Cloud enabled, in the customer’s account in Shelly Smart Control.** The app reaches them through the plug only via the cloud. A device that runs locally has to be moved on its own, through its own access point.

## Set up the plug at handover

### 1. Open the plug’s web interface

Plug it in. On your phone, join the plug’s own network — its name starts with *ShellyPlug* — and open `http://192.168.33.1`

 ![Web interface of a Shelly Plug M Gen3 at 192.168.33.1, asking to configure Wi-Fi](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-01-web-home.webp) 

### 2. Connect it to the customer’s Wi-Fi

Under **Wi-Fi 1 settings**, turn on **Enable Wi-Fi**, choose the customer’s network, enter the password, and press **Save**.

 ![Wi-Fi 1 settings in the plug's web interface, connected to the customer's network](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-02-wifi-settings.webp) 

### 3. Update the firmware

*Settings → Firmware* — install the latest firmware.

### 4. Copy the customer’s network — last

Under **Access point & range extender settings**, set **SSID** and **Password** to the customer’s Wi-Fi name and password, exactly as they are, capitals included. Tick **Enable range extender** and press **Save**.

 ![Access point and range extender settings with the customer's network name as SSID and range extender enabled](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-03-access-point.webp) 

This is the last step because it makes the plug look like the customer’s router. Your phone loses the connection when you save.

### 5. Unplug it and put it away

Unplug it straight away. Fill in the customer sheet and leave the plug in a fixed place at the customer’s home together with it.

## Use it after a router change

### 1. Plug it in and open the plug

Plug it into a socket. Connect your phone to the **OLD** network name with the **OLD** password – that is now the plug. Your phone may say “no internet” – choose to stay connected. Open `192.168.33.1`.

### 2. Connect the plug to the new network

Under **Wi-Fi 1 settings**, choose the new network, enter its password, and press **Save**.

 ![Wi-Fi 1 settings in the plug's web interface, used to connect the plug to the new network](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-02-wifi-settings.webp) 

The old devices now go online through the plug.

### 3. Open Wi-Fi batch change

In Shelly Smart Control, open a room, open the room’s menu, and tap **Wi-Fi batch change**.

 ![Room menu in Shelly Smart Control with Wi-Fi batch change under Device actions](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-04-batch-menu.webp) 

### 4. Select the devices

Tap **Select All**, then **Change**.

 ![Device list in a room with devices ticked and the Change button](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-05-select-devices.webp) 

### 5. Choose Wi-Fi 1

Choose **Wi-Fi 1** and tap **Next**.

 ![Select Wi-Fi dialog with Wi-Fi 1 chosen](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-06-wifi1-or-2.webp) 

The app recommends Wi-Fi 2. Here it has to be Wi-Fi 1. With Wi-Fi 2, a device stays on the plug for as long as it can see the old network, and its place on the plug is never freed.

### 6. Enter the new network

Enter the new network’s name and password, and tap **Save**.

 ![Wi-Fi credentials dialog with the new network's name and password and the Save button](/assets/img/screenshots/wifi-rescue-07-new-network.webp) 

Do **one device first** and watch it come online on the new network before you move the rest. A device with a static IP address is switched to DHCP, as the app says.

### 7. Repeat room by room

Do the next room the same way. Carry the plug closer to devices it does not reach.

### 8. Ready the plug for next time

When every device has moved, set the plug’s **SSID** and **Password** to the **new** network’s name and password, as in step 4 of the setup. Unplug it and put it back with an updated note.

## Limits

* **2.4 GHz only.** The plug and the devices use 2.4 GHz, so the new router has to offer it.
* **A network name of 29 characters or fewer.** That is the longest name the plug’s access point can take.
* **A password of at least 8 characters.**
* **A limited number of devices at a time** — typically 4–5. It matters less than it sounds: a device leaves the plug as soon as it has the new network, and the next one can join.

## How it works

A Shelly does not remember a router. It remembers a Wi-Fi name and a password, and connects to whatever answers to them. If the new router keeps the old name and password, the devices reconnect on their own and the plug is not needed.

Every Shelly also runs its own access point. *Range extender* lets other Shelly devices join that access point and reach the internet through the plug’s own Wi-Fi connection. With the old name and password on the access point, the old devices take the plug for their router.

[Shelly to Shelly without Wi-Fi](/en/application-guide/shelly-to-shelly-without-wifi/) uses the same two functions to run devices with no router at all.
