# DIY Home Alarm: Smart Security Without a Monitoring Contract

[Love0](javascript:; "Love")

[![DIY Home Alarm: Smart Security Without a Monitoring Contract](https://smarterhome.sk/stupload/stblog/1/182/415/182415large.jpg)](https://smarterhome.sk/en/blog/diy-home-alarm-smart-security-without-monitoring-contract_182.html "DIY Home Alarm: Smart Security Without a Monitoring Contract")

A **DIY home alarm** is something you can build yourself today: wireless door and window sensors, motion sensors, a camera, a siren and a hub that pushes an alert to your phone the moment something happens. No cables through the whole flat, no installer and no monthly contract with a security company. Most devices stick on with double-sided tape, so one afternoon is enough to have your home protected.

In this guide we compare three approaches that make sense: the [Aqara](https://smarterhome.sk/en/aqara_71) ecosystem with a hub that has a built-in siren, the affordable and simple [SONOFF](https://smarterhome.sk/en/sonoff_98), and open Home Assistant with sensors and a keypad from [frient](https://smarterhome.sk/en/frient_81). For those who just want a camera with a siren we also mention [Netatmo](https://smarterhome.sk/en/netatmo_46), and Z‑Wave for large houses.

You will learn how to build layered security (doors and windows → motion → camera → siren → notification), where to put the motion sensor and the siren, how a smart alarm behaves during a power or internet outage, what your insurer may say about a DIY alarm, and when a monitoring centre still makes sense. At the end you will find ready-made bundles for a 3-room flat and a family house, with prices.

## **DIY home alarm vs. classic alarm vs. monitoring centre**

All three do the same job: detect an intrusion and alert someone. They differ in who installs the system, who responds to an alarm and how much you pay each month.

On mobile, swipe the table sideways to see all columns.

| DIY smart alarm | Classic alarm (wired panel) | Alarm + monitoring centre |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Upfront cost** | **€140–560 flat, €280–900 house** (our bundles below) | €400–700 flat, €700–1,500 house incl. installation | as classic alarm + communicator |
| **Monthly** | **€0** (camera cloud optional) | €0 without monitoring | single to low double digits of euros, call-outs often extra |
| **Installation** | yourself, adhesive tape, no cables | installer, often wired sensors | installer |
| **Who responds** | you, family, neighbour | you (siren, app, SMS) | monitoring operator and response unit |
| **Expansion** | lights, lock, heating, water in one app | security only | security only |
| **Power cut** | battery sensors yes, hub and router need a UPS | battery in the panel | battery in the panel |
| **Internet outage** | siren works locally, notifications don’t | GSM communicator | GSM / LTE / radio |
| **Insurance** | depends on the terms, often lower tiers only | recognised as an alarm system | highest tiers |

Prices for a classic alarm are quoted, for example, by the Bratislava installer SECURE (August 2026): typically €400–700 for a flat and €700–1,500 for a family house, including installation. According to the same company, monitoring is billed separately, at roughly single to low double digits of euros per month. Just as a rough guide: an older price list of one Slovak security company showed €35 a month plus €25 per call-out, or €50 with two call-outs included. Current prices vary by company and region.

### **When a DIY smart alarm is enough**

A flat, a family house without extremely valuable items, a cottage, a rented flat. You want to know about every door opening, scare off a burglar with a siren and check on the camera what is going on. You respond to the alarm yourself – call a neighbour or the police (158 in Slovakia and Czechia, 112 anywhere in the EU).

### **When to consider a classic alarm**

A new build before plastering, where cables are easy to run, or a house where you want a certified system to meet your insurer’s requirements. A classic alarm panel has a battery and a GSM communicator built in.

### **When a monitoring centre pays off**

When you don’t want to deal with an alarm at 3 a.m. while on holiday, you keep art or collections at home, or your insurer requires a monitored connection for a high sum insured. A smart alarm and professional monitoring are not mutually exclusive – the cameras and sensors in this guide can run alongside monitoring for convenience and insight.

## **How layered security works**

A good alarm never relies on a single sensor. Each layer catches something different, and when one fails, the next one picks it up. Professional systems distinguish perimeter protection (doors and windows) from space protection (motion inside) – a smart home works the same way, just without cables.

[![Diagram of 5 security layers in a flat: doors and windows, motion, camera, siren and notification with a hub and UPS](https://smarterhome.sk/img/cms/Navody_a_tipy/182-alarm/alarm-schema-vrstvy-EN.webp)](https://smarterhome.sk/img/cms/Navody_a_tipy/182-alarm/alarm-schema-vrstvy-EN.webp) 

Diagram: the five layers of a smart alarm and a hub on a UPS. Click to enlarge.

 

1. **Perimeter** – contact sensors on doors and windows, vibration sensors on glass. The alarm goes off before the burglar is inside.
2. **Motion inside** – a PIR sensor in the hallway or living room catches anyone who came in another way.
3. **Camera** – verifies whether it’s a real break-in or just the cat and a draught. The footage helps the police and your insurer.
4. **Siren** – loud sound and light disturb the burglar and alert the neighbours.
5. **Notification** – an alert to your phone and your family’s, ideally with a camera snapshot.

On top of it all runs the hub with arming modes: when you are home only doors and windows are armed, when you leave motion is armed too, and at night only the ground floor.

## **Layer 1: doors and windows**

The perimeter is the most important layer. A burglar gets in through a door or a window – and a contact sensor reacts the moment it opens, before anything can be taken.

Without a monitoring centre, you are the response unit. That’s why the alert must arrive quickly, reach several people and contain enough information for you to decide what to do.

### **One person’s push notification is not enough**

Invite your partner, adult children or the neighbour who lives closest into the app. In Home Assistant, set up critical notifications that break through Do Not Disturb and attach a camera snapshot to the alert. ## **Power and internet outages**

This is the most common question about DIY alarms, and rightly so. A classic alarm panel has a battery and a GSM module. A smart system can cope too, but you have to plan for it.

### **Power cut**

Battery sensors (doors, windows, motion, vibration) keep working. The problem is the hub, router and mains-powered cameras. The solution is a small UPS for the router and hub – a DC UPS with a 5 V or 12 V output is enough – or a PoE switch on a UPS that keeps the M3 hub, the G5 Pro camera and the SLZB-06U coordinator alive at once.

Choose a siren with a backup battery ([frient](https://smarterhome.sk/en/alarms/frient-smart-siren-eu-sirzb-111-zigbee-siren-1275.html), [HEIMAN](https://smarterhome.sk/en/alarms/heiman-zigbee-smart-indoor-siren-hs2wd-zb-1387.html), [SONOFF SNZB-09P](https://smarterhome.sk/en/alarms/sonoff-snzb-09p-senseguard-zigbee-indoor-siren-2413.html)) or a fully battery-powered one ([Philio PSE04](https://smarterhome.sk/en/alarms/philio-pse04-z-wave-outdoor-battery-siren-ip44-2442.html)). The battery helps when a burglar unplugs the siren, but the siren only sounds while the hub is running – which is why a UPS for the hub matters more than a battery in the siren. The frient siren also sends an alert by itself when it loses power.

### **Internet outage**

If the hub evaluates the alarm locally (Aqara M3 and M200, SONOFF Bridge Pro and Max, Home Assistant), the siren sounds even without internet and cameras keep recording to their own storage. What doesn’t work are phone notifications – those need a connection.

For peace of mind, use a router with LTE failover (a SIM card takes over when the fixed line drops), or at least enable a cloud alert when the hub goes offline so you know about the outage. A burglar who cuts the cable outside will take down your internet, but not your siren.

### **Jamming and tampering**

Zigbee and Wi‑Fi can in theory be jammed, so prefer hubs that report a lost connection to a sensor. Don’t put the hub and router right next to the front door, where a burglar would find and rip them out first – the middle of the flat or a utility room is better.

## **What your insurer may say about a smart alarm**

Home contents insurance covers burglary, but insurers tie the payout limit to the level of security. For lower sums insured, good doors and locks are usually enough; for higher ones, insurers add a requirement for an electronic alarm system or monitoring.

### **What the terms look like**

Just as a rough guide to what such terms look like: older home contents terms of the Slovak insurer Kooperativa distinguish three levels of alarm: with local sound and light signalling, with an automatic phone dialler to at least two numbers, and with a connection to a monitoring centre. A functional alarm is defined as a system that responds to an intrusion with a siren, light or a signal to a phone and has a backup power supply for at least 48 hours. The current terms of your own policy may differ.

According to an overview by the Slovak portal Finsider (2023), Allianz requires space protection with an alarm connected to a monitoring centre for sums above €50,000; Uniqa requires an alarm signalling to at least two phones from €100,000 and a connection to an alarm receiving centre above €200,000. Generali does not require an alarm but raises limits when you have one. Insurers in other countries work in a similar way.

### **When a DIY alarm helps**

A smart alarm with a siren and notifications to two phones functionally comes close to the lower alarm levels. What counts, however, are your insurer’s specific terms. Some only give a discount for a certified alarm connected to a monitoring centre, and an ordinary hub without a UPS won’t meet a 48-hour backup requirement.

**What to do:** before signing (or when changing) your policy, send the insurer a description of your system and ask for written confirmation of the security level it will be classed as. And remember that mechanical security – a security door and cylinder – is still the basis for insurance that no sensor can replace.

## **Which system for which situation**

### **Upper-floor flat in an apartment block**

The most common way in is the front door. A door sensor on the entrance, a motion sensor in the hallway and a siren are enough. The Flat – Starter bundle or Aqara with the M200 hub will do the job, and a smart lock adds automatic arming.

### **Ground-floor flat and family house**

Here the whole perimeter makes sense: a sensor on every window and door, vibration sensors on large glazing and the patio, an outdoor camera above the entrance and a siren that can be heard outside. For a house, consider PoE – one cable powers the camera and hub, and with a UPS they survive a power cut.

### **Holiday cottage**

Often without permanent internet and with power cuts. Choose a hub with local logic, a battery-powered siren, an LTE router and sensors with long battery life (frient Entry Sensor 2 Pro). A water leak sensor and a frost thermometer are useful too.

### **Rented flat**

No drilling: sensors and sirens stick on, the hub plugs into a socket. When you move, peel everything off and take it with you. A smart lock that fits onto the existing cylinder (Aqara U200, Nuki) can also be removed without a trace – just agree it with your landlord.

### **Apple household**

The Aqara M3, M200 and G5 Pro work with Apple Home, Aqara sensors appear in the Home app via the hub, and the U200 lock supports Apple Home Key. Most security features (modes, delays) are still set up in the Aqara Home app.

### **Home Assistant**

Mix brands by price and features: SONOFF sensors, frient keypad and siren, Aqara vibration sensors, cameras via RTSP. Alarmo gives the system the logic of a classic alarm panel, and everything runs locally. Our article [Shelly vs SONOFF vs Aqara](https://smarterhome.sk/en/blog/shelly-vs-sonoff-vs-aqara-which-ecosystem_180.html) helps you choose a brand.

## **Common mistakes**

**No entry delay.** The siren sounds before you can disarm. Set 15–30 seconds on the front door and let windows and motion trigger instantly.

**Motion sensor facing a window or radiator.** Sunlight, curtains in a draught and warm air cause false alarms. After the first week, check the history and adjust sensitivity.

**Siren and hub right by the door.** A burglar will find them in seconds. Put them in the middle of the home, high up and out of sight.

**No backup power.** Without a UPS for the router and hub, a power cut takes down notifications and cameras. Battery sensors alone are not enough.

**An untested system.** Run a test alarm once a month, check batteries and make sure everyone receives the notifications. A sensor with a flat battery protects nothing.

## **Frequently asked questions**

### **Is a DIY home alarm as reliable as a classic alarm?**

With a good design, yes – if the sensors run on batteries, the hub evaluates the alarm locally, and the router and hub have backup power. A classic alarm panel has the advantage of certification and a built-in battery and GSM module. A smart alarm wins on price, easy installation and integration with cameras, a lock and lights.

### **How much does an alarm for a flat cost without a security company contract?**

The basic SONOFF bundle with a siren costs €139.94, a full Aqara bundle with a camera €361.90, and €561.88 with a smart lock added. You pay nothing monthly unless you want cloud storage for cameras. A classic installed alarm costs roughly €400–700 according to installers.

### **Does a smart alarm work during an internet outage?**

The siren and local automations do, if the hub has local logic – for example the Aqara M3 and M200, SONOFF Bridge Pro and Max, or Home Assistant. Phone notifications don’t work until the connection is restored. A router with LTE failover solves this.

### **What happens during a power cut?**

Battery sensors keep working, and the frient, HEIMAN and SONOFF SNZB-09P sirens have a backup battery. The hub, router and cameras, however, need a UPS or a PoE switch on backup power. Without it the hub can’t process an alarm during a power cut, and the siren’s battery alone won’t help.

### **Will my insurer accept a DIY alarm?**

It depends on the insurer and the sum insured. For lower sums, mechanical security is enough; for higher ones, insurers require an alarm system, often with 48-hour backup power, or a monitored connection. Send your insurer a description of the system in advance and ask for written confirmation.

### **Does an alarm have to be installed by a licensed company?**

You can install a smart alarm in your own flat or house yourself. In Slovakia, Act No. 473/2005 classes designing, installing and maintaining alarm systems as a technical service that licence holders provide as a business. If you want a discount or higher limits from your insurer, check whether they require a system installed by a licensed company.

### **What if I have a dog or a cat?**

Use a pet-immune motion sensor such as the frient Motion Sensor 2 Pet, which ignores pets up to 25 kg. Or, in Away mode, arm motion only in rooms the pet doesn’t enter and leave the rest of the house to the door and vibration sensors.

### **How do I arm the alarm without forgetting?**

Let it arm automatically: based on your phones’ location, when the smart lock is locked from outside, or on a schedule. For other household members, the frient keypad with an RFID tag or a button by the door is simple. A “nobody is home and the alarm is off” notification is a good safety net.

### **Do I need Home Assistant for an alarm?**

No. Aqara and SONOFF have arming modes in their apps and work without a server. Home Assistant is worth it if you want to mix brands, write your own rules and use a keypad like on a classic alarm panel.

### **How long should the siren sound, and how do I keep the neighbours happy?**

We recommend a few minutes, for example 3, then silence with ongoing phone alerts. For less serious events, use a quieter tone or just the light. Let your neighbours know you have an alarm and give them your contact – they are your closest “response unit”.

For a family house, the strongest choice is **Aqara with the G5 Pro camera and U200 lock**, and for tech enthusiasts **Home Assistant with Alarmo, the frient keypad and siren**. Don’t forget a UPS for the router and hub, and written confirmation from your insurer.
